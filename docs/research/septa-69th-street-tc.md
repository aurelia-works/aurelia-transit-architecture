# SEPTA 69th Street Transportation Center (Upper Darby, PA): reference facts

Facts only, each with a source. "unknown" = not found in the sources checked (2026-10-20). Most physical detail is from Wikipedia's infobox and a 2015-16 West Terminal renovation release.

| Topic | Fact | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Address / owner | 6901 Market Street, Upper Darby Township, PA (Terminal Square); owner SEPTA | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/69th_Street_Transportation_Center |
| Lines | Market-Frankford (L), Norristown High Speed Line (M), Route D trolley; 14 SEPTA bus routes (City and Suburban) | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/69th_Street_Transportation_Center |
| Structure (infobox) | At-grade (Wikipedia infobox). Note: the page also says the L platforms are "below-grade" with stairs or lifts from the main level, so the classification is inconsistent | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/69th_Street_Transportation_Center |
| Platforms (infobox) | L: 1 island and 1 side, 3 tracks. D: 1 island and 1 side, 2 tracks. M: 2 island and 2 side, 3 tracks | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/69th_Street_Transportation_Center |
| Platform numbers / length / width | unknown | - |
| L level | Three tracks; westbound trains end on the north track; eastbound trains use a balloon loop to reach the two southern tracks, which straddle the loading platform. Trains continue west to a shared yard | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/69th_Street_Transportation_Center |
| M terminal | All three tracks terminate facing the station, platforms both sides. Sits between L tracks and North Terminal bus bays | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/69th_Street_Transportation_Center |
| West Terminal (north of Market St) | Clockwise inner loop to the central terminal; counter-clockwise outer loop with two boarding areas used by D trolleys; three platforms connect indoors to the terminal; unpaved track on northern platform | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/69th_Street_Transportation_Center |
| North Terminal | Sheltered berths in a line, two bus turnaround loops; reached by private SEPTA road from Victory Avenue | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/69th_Street_Transportation_Center |
| South Terminal | Buses in from Market St east, out west; long canopied platform; smaller island with shelter; taxi stand north | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/69th_Street_Transportation_Center |
| Roofs / canopies | West Terminal: platform canopies rebuilt in 2016; green roof and LED lighting added. Other canopy shape, material, colour, height: unknown | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/69th_Street_Transportation_Center ; https://www.masstransitmag.com/rail/press-release/12064240/southeastern-pennsylvania-transportation-authority-septa-septa-breaks-ground-on-69th-street-west-terminal-renovations |
| Concourse | Great Hall (1907) is the main building. Concourse size and level relative to tracks: unknown. Access from main level by stairs or elevators | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/69th_Street_Transportation_Center |
| Gates | April 2024: new fare gates at L and M platform entrances (per a cited article) | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/69th_Street_Transportation_Center |
| Escalators / lifts | Elevators mentioned; escalator count unknown | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/69th_Street_Transportation_Center |
| Building | Great Hall: brick, arched windows, opened 1907 (infobox photo, black and white). Style, storeys, exact materials: unknown. 1962 three-track terminal with air-conditioned waiting room replaced the original wooden station. Shops and cinema added 1936 | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/69th_Street_Transportation_Center ; infobox screenshot |
| Parking | 186 spaces (lot east of station); 318-berth garage above South Terminal announced 2018, status not stated | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/69th_Street_Transportation_Center |
| Footbridge | Pedestrian steel footbridge over the platforms, expanded 2020 (caption) | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/69th_Street_Transportation_Center (infobox screenshot) |
| Passengers | 35,000 per day (2019); second-busiest SEPTA transfer after 15th St / City Hall | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/69th_Street_Transportation_Center |
| History | Opened 4 March 1907 as western terminus of Market Street Elevated; 1936 loop; 1962 terminal; 2015-16 West Terminal renovation ($19.6 million; FTA and Act 89 funded) | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/69th_Street_Transportation_Center ; https://www.masstransitmag.com/rail/press-release/12064240/southeastern-pennsylvania-transportation-authority-septa-septa-breaks-ground-on-69th-street-west-terminal-renovations |
| Signage / branding | L, D, M line roundels (L blue, D pink, M purple); SEPTA logo and route icons | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/69th_Street_Transportation_Center (infobox screenshot) |

## Minecraft notes

1. Build the L platform as a below-grade trench with stairs and lifts up to a main level, not an elevated structure. Mark the grade inconsistency in the build notes.
2. Use a 1907-style brick Great Hall with arched windows at the main entrance, and a footbridge over the tracks.
3. Put the North, South and West terminals as separate bus areas with canopied bays and a green roof on the West Terminal.
4. Show the balloon loop for L eastbound and the outer loop for D trolleys, with three platforms for M terminal.
5. Use L, D and M line roundels in blue, pink and purple above platform entrances.
