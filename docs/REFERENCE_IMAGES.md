# Station Generator — real-world reference images

Commons file pages below were fetched and returned HTTP 200 on 17 October 2026; each page links to the original image and states its own attribution and licence. The author and licence are transcribed from the file page. Link to the file page rather than hot-linking an image when reusing it, and retain the indicated attribution/licence terms.

**Scale key:** treat a block as roughly a metre for proportion, not exact survey. The generator's entrance building is 9 blocks deep; compact/city/grand buildings are two/three/four storeys (about 4 blocks per storey). Standard platform canopies keep 4 blocks clear below girders at y=4 and roof at y=5; hall-shed ribs use station girders, roof spans are kept to 8 blocks or less, and an arched shed rises to about y=8 (about y=11 over a footbridge or building). Adapt references to the straight ground-level footprints the generator actually builds.

The hall and exterior show Utrecht's low, light-filled glass-and-concrete envelope; the platform view is a useful contemporary canopy and wayfinding reference. Translate with `metro_pale_concrete`, `curtain_wall_glass`, `utrecht_light_grey_floor_tile`, square steel columns, and a shallow stepped wave roof; keep the glazed street wall 2–3 storeys tall, with a 9-block-deep entrance hall and a broad 8–12-block public span. **Concourse anatomy:** street ramp → glazed ticket hall and ticket machines → transverse fare-gate line → paid hall → stair/lift to the footbridge → platform stairs. Keep the entrance legible through the glass; the station hall is a public forecourt, not an opaque monumental vestibule.

The references show the dense Gothic sandstone-and-stone facade, central dome/tower, turrets and the railway-side platform environment. Use Frankfurt sandstone ashlar, ornate limestone bands, polished-andesite plinth, marble floor and clear glass; build a 3–4-storey street block with one dominant pointed entrance, a raised clock/dome tower and smaller corner turrets, translating curved profiles into stepped stairs/slabs. Use a simpler steel-and-glass platform canopy on 6–8-block bays (the street facade is the ornate element), with stone or steel columns outside clearance, ATA benches and lights, and MTR ticket machines, tactile map, gates and high departure boards inside the hall.

**Concourse anatomy:** street doors under the pointed arch → ticket hall → transverse gate line → paid concourse → stair/lift or footbridge connections to the suburban platforms. Keep the street threshold broad, place ticketing on the public side, and use platform number signs at the gate and every stair landing.

## Gaps and interpretation notes

- All 18 looks have at least three verified, freely reusable Commons file-page references (55 in total); the photos vary in how clearly they show interior furniture or signage, so use them primarily as architectural/layout evidence and retain the generator's own MTR/ATA equipment placement.
- Grand Central's rail platforms are underground, so there is no comparable above-ground train shed to copy. The entry hall, concourse and facade are directly referenced; the generator-scale platform roof should be treated as a separate fictionalized covered-platform solution, not a literal Grand Central shed.
- Kuala Lumpur's selected open references are especially strong for its distinctive exterior; a good freely licensed interior/platform-shed photo of the old station was not located. The suggested platform roof is a practical Minecraft interpretation and should not be read as a measured reconstruction.
- The St Pancras references are good for the Barlow shed and Gothic frontage, but a clear, freely licensed ticket-hall/gate-line view was not located in this pass. Amsterdam's interior reference is a stair/escalator connection rather than a full ticket-hall overview.
- St Pancras, Antwerp, Frankfurt, Tokyo, Milan, Paris and Mumbai are not dimensionally surveyed here. The block spans/heights above are deliberate generator-scale abstractions tied to ATA's existing 8-block support/span and 9-block building-depth constraints, not claims about exact real measurements.
